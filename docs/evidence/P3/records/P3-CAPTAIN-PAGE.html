Question 3 · BOOM 55.5–75 s, about 138 BPM

Does the light carry the beat?

factThe beat test was frozen before its final run, but after the iteration-3 reviewer's per-series numbers were read, so its "frozen" status adds little beyond its planted check. The analysis script was edited after a first run; the criterion and the planted recipe did not change. The test: the best repeat period lies within 0.425–0.445 s, and at least 8% of rhythmic power sits at the beat. In practice the period clause decides every verdict. On a synthetic signal it finds a planted beat and rejects the 3+3+2 accent pattern alone at the camera's and the host's sampling rates (40 of 40 seeds, reviewer). At the camera's 30 Hz it sees a beat reliably only when the beat holds about 13% or more of the power, and never at about 8% (reviewer's amplitude sweep). At the audio's 20 Hz it found the planted beat in only 42% of seeds (reviewer), so it gives no verdict on the audio itself. The audio's spectrum still peaks at 2.30 Hz, with 17% of its rhythmic power there.

post hocBand shares cannot separate a once-per-bar line from broadband slow variation, which piles up at low rates. A line test (peak against its neighbours, with off-grid control rates) shows a bar line on the Hybrid K1 board only: 4.5 and 6.2 times its neighbours, against controls up to 1.8 and 3.1. On the Tempo board the bar reads 1.3 and 2.1 against controls of 1.3 and 1.5: no line. The Tempo board's beat line is strong (4.6 and 6.1). The camera lower band's 23% bar share is mostly slow variation. Whether it holds a bar line depends on the test: 4.4 times its neighbours (controls up to 1.6), but only 2.4 times a fitted slow-variation floor, against 2.6 off-grid (reviewer). Not claimed.

factIteration 3 compared the light with a signal built from rises in loudness, labelled "music onsets". That signal is accent-weighted and holds only 0.2% of its power at the beat, against 17% for plain loudness. With it, the lower-edge camera band scores the same with the light 367 ms ahead (p 0.001), so that match is shared rhythm, not a response. With loudness as the reference, the match holds only with the light behind.

factThe band near the upper edge follows neither reference (p 0.82–0.96; one light-leading test reaches p 0.02, out of 24 run). The repeat-period scan finds no musical period in it (best 0.51 s). It holds 8% of its power at the half-time rate, or 14% after a 1 s moving mean. Which board it shows is not established.

post hocThe lower-edge band has a small line at 2.30 Hz, 3.5 times its neighbours, where off-grid control rates reach at most 1.6. It carries 3% of the band's power and fails the frozen test, so it is not claimed as the beat.

inferenceWhether the host's beat survives emission, the plate and the 24/255 cap is open. A 30 fps camera with automatic exposure is a weak witness for a 2.3 Hz pulse.

factThe 67 ms is where the correlation peaks. It includes the iPhone's own audio–video alignment and the camera's 33 ms frame step, so it is not a measured delay of the Titan.

factThe tempo replay was run on three inputs (the song file at gains 0.7–1.6, plus the room recording):

  • Verse, 7–33 s: 69 BPM, which is half-time.
  • 33–43 s: about 138.
  • First 1.5 s of the drop: about 112, with the gate nearly closed.
  • 55.5–75 s: mostly 92 (two-thirds of 138) or 69. The room recording reads 92 for 84% of the drop and is never near 138. The song file reaches 138 for 15–16% of the drop, and only at gain 1.155 or above.

inferenceThe tracker seems to lock onto a sub-multiple or the accent grouping rather than the pulse. Half-time counts as a correct tempo by the usual tolerances; whether it looks wrong on the plate is your call.

factIn true silence after Dopex the AGC output sits at music level, so the darkness comes from gates, not from the level. The gates that can force darkness are the silence flag, the spectral noise gate, the tonality gate and the donor admission gate, as listed in the trace.

Don't judge brightness from these videos

The iPhone sets its exposure automatically. Going into and out of silence, it brightens the picture as the boards darken. Decoded, the camera spans about 0.7–3 dB where the payload spans 4–11 dB. For brightness, trust the payload.
